How the Cornerstone works

Each step below is a section of the Cornerstone Livelihood Restoration Act. {% if is_preview() %}In the preview you can perform every step yourself, in any role, at Try the preview.{% endif %}

  1. An event is certified (section 2)

    Two affected workers, a union, an employer, a state workforce agency or a governor petitions the Department of Labor. The petition is published, and everyone has at least 60 days to submit evidence. Labor certifies the event only if destroying or shrinking the industry was an express objective or an intended means of the government action, the action was carried out, and it directly caused the loss of identifiable people's livelihoods. Foreseeable harm alone is not enough, and ordinary market change never qualifies.

  2. Each worker proves an individual claim (section 3)

    A worker shows that the covered work was their principal livelihood (more than half of earned income, or at least 1,000 hours in a representative year) and that they lost it within the certified period. Later jobs, pensions, severance and unemployment benefits do not erase or reduce the claim. A claim survives death and passes, undivided, to one successor.

  3. Workers form a Cornerstone Association (section 4)

    Certified workers form a worker-owned association before any opportunity is assigned. Enrollment stays open for 180 days and then closes, so no one can join later to chase a benefit. Every founder holds one vote and an equal share. Rankings, the compact and other major decisions need a majority of all founders. Membership cannot be sold, and outside investors can never take control.

  4. States publish offers on the public Exchange (sections 5 and 6)

    A participating state, under its own enabling law, offers a district in a trade that is prohibited today. It must prove on a public record that no existing job, license, contract, customer or market is taken. Offers stay public for at least 90 days.

  5. Associations rank the offers in secret, and the match is published (section 5)

    Each association's members approve a ranked list that stays sealed until the common deadline. The Department of Commerce runs a published matching rule. When two associations want the same offer, the one representing the oldest unremedied injury has priority; equal dates are ordered by one public random drawing. Size, wealth, residence and politics give no advantage. Anyone can reproduce the result.

  6. The association negotiates and approves a compact (section 6)

    The compact grants the regional right for 30 years, with one automatic 10-year renewal while the workers keep control. An independent state supervisor must approve material pricing, access, service, safety and exclusion decisions.

  7. The Founding Fund advances start-up costs, and is repaid (section 8)

    The Fund advances only organizing, feasibility, legal, licensing and training costs. The enterprise repays the money actually advanced plus a fixed public return, as a small fixed percentage of its receipts, and then the obligation ends permanently. No worker ever owes it personally.

  8. Labor confirms the restoration (section 9)

    The claim is marked remedied only when the association has an enforceable compact, every legal authorization, a feasibility finding, funded training, a path to capital, and operations have begun. If government later defeats the compact, the claims reopen.
